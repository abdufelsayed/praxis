# Product Discovery Collaboration

Load this reference when a Praxis owner is resolving product, domain,
package, API, developer-experience, working-form, specification, or shared
strategic meaning with the requester. It defines a collaboration posture, not
another workflow, record, or gate.

## Work as a product pair

Start at the level of the product being built. For a package or developer
tool, frame scenarios around the package consumer, extension author, runtime
adapter, or maintainer. Do not silently answer as an application developer or
turn configurable package behavior into application policy.

The requester owns intended experience, taste, priority, acceptable tradeoffs,
and consequential product meaning. The active owner brings evidence, exposes
blind spots, turns abstractions into concrete examples, compares real options,
and recommends a direction with reasons. Neither side substitutes for the
other.

Research findings, technical possibilities, and solution recommendations are
candidate inputs, not accepted meaning. Turn them into a concrete consequence
for the product or strategy, discuss that consequence with the requester, and
record only the meaning the requester actually accepts. Do not let an owner
interpret evidence into product truth alone.

Use the vocabulary already established in the project. Explain an unfamiliar
term through a small example before asking for a judgment based on it.

## Move from evidence to judgment

Substantial research improves visibility but does not select product meaning.
Before asking the requester to judge its implications, present a compact
transition brief:

- the product meaning currently understood;
- uncertainty that still matters;
- judgments that are ready;
- the dependency order between those judgments; and
- the recommended first topic with its reason.

Show a provisional topic map when several judgments remain. The requester
selects the transition and first topic. Do not treat the research conclusion,
recommended topic, or topic order as accepted product meaning.

## Keep one coherent conversation

Lead with the actual question. Add the smallest concrete scenario or
pseudo-interface that makes the consequence visible. Present material
alternatives only when they expose a real choice. Then give a recommendation
and its reason. Do not force ordinary conversation into labeled questionnaire
prose.

Ask dependent questions one at a time because an answer may change the next
question, options, or recommendation. A small set of independent,
already-grounded judgments may use one Plannotator bundle. A required gate
always uses its owning Plannotator path; conversational agreement is not a gate
substitute.

Within one agreed inquiry, continue to the next dependent question after
integrating an answer unless the requester pauses or redirects. This is
conversation continuity, not authority to persist, switch owners, open a gate,
or take an effect.

An individual approval settles only the current conversational meaning. It
does not authorize persistence, a gate, another topic, or implementation.

When a correction arrives, stop the current framing, state the corrected
understanding plainly, and repair affected provisional conclusions before
continuing. Do not defend or extend a model the requester has rejected.
State the mistaken assumption, the meaning that remains valid, the affected
meaning, and the revised topic map when the correction changes later questions.

## Persist coherent meaning, not conversation

Keep provisional answers in the live conversation while a coherent topic is
still changing. Do not write after every small approval. At a natural topic
closeout, reconcile:

- accepted product meaning;
- corrected or rejected interpretations;
- research findings and their disposition;
- Requirements supported by the accepted meaning;
- genuine uncertainty that remains; and
- the next consequential question.

Persist that cluster together only when persistence is already authorized or
after one explicit persistence check. Approval of a product judgment alone is
not file-edit authority. A request to discuss, pause, not start, or not edit
permits no tool use, persistence, delegation, gate, or other effect unless the
same direction explicitly asks for a bounded read-only check.
