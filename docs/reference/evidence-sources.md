# Industry source index

> **Status: research catalog, not normative Praxis authority.**
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-07-29. “Company report” means the organization is
> describing its own system or results. It may be useful primary evidence
> without being independent validation.

This index records the articles behind the
[industry research map](evidence-landscape.md), including the links collected by *Why
Software Factories Fail*. It distinguishes sources reviewed in depth from
material retained for context. It catalogs every substantive article,
benchmark, method, talk, and example linked by the post. Authored visual
material is included when it carries part of the argument; author-profile,
contact, company-signup, and decorative asset links are excluded.

## Benchmarks, training, and verification

| Source | Type and review status | Relevance and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| [SWE-Agent paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793) | Research paper/preprint; reviewed at findings level. | Shows that agent-computer interface design materially changes performance. It does not test long-term maintainability. |
| [SlopCodeBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24755), [official site](https://www.scbench.ai/), [runner](https://github.com/SprocketLab/slop-code-bench), and [problem set](https://github.com/SprocketLab/scb-problems) | Research preprint, benchmark implementation, and dataset; current paper revision and public repositories reviewed in depth. | Evaluates agents across sequentially revealed external contracts while carrying forward each agent's own workspace and prior requirements as regression tests. The current paper reports 36 problems and 196 checkpoints, a 14.8% best strict pass rate, and no fully solved problem. Its deterministic erosion and verbosity measures are trajectory signals, not a complete maintainability oracle; exact headline counts differ across paper revisions, so results must name the revision used. |
| [Telos: Killing Coding Agent Slop With Adversarial Self-Play](https://usetelos.ai/blog/killing-coding-agent-slop) and [Telos repository](https://github.com/telos-org/telos) | Company experiment and open-source implementation responding directly to SlopCodeBench; article and repository reviewed in depth. | Proposes a generator paired with an independent adversarial verifier working from the same durable goal specification. | [SWE-bench Multilingual](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-bench_Multilingual) | Public benchmark dataset; inspected. | Its bounded `FAIL_TO_PASS` and `PASS_TO_PASS` grading illustrates why functional correctness is easier to reward than design quality. |

| [Mutation testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_testing) | Background reference; catalogued. | Explains how tests can be checked for their ability to detect changed behavior. It measures test strength, not architecture by itself. |
| [Awesome RLVR](https://github.com/opendilab/awesome-RLVR) | Curated resource list; catalogued. | Background on reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, not direct evidence about software-factory outcomes. |
| [OpenAI Codex talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wVl6ZjELpBk) | First-party talk; catalogued. | Supports the claim that model training and harness design interact. No independent maintainability result follows from it. |

## In-Context Reinforcement Learning and contextual adaptation

| Source | Type and review status | Relevance and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Nathan Lambert's [RLHF Book](https://rlhfbook.com/), especially [Nature of Preferences](https://rlhfbook.com/c/10-preferences), [Preference Data](https://rlhfbook.com/c/11-preference-data), [Reward Modeling](https://rlhfbook.com/c/05-reward-models), [Policy Gradients](https://rlhfbook.com/c/06-policy-gradients), [Over-Optimization](https://rlhfbook.com/c/14-over-optimization), [Regularization](https://rlhfbook.com/c/15-regularization), and [Evaluation](https://rlhfbook.com/c/16-evaluation) | Author-maintained technical book and primary synthesis; two bounded research passes reviewed the relevant chapters and decisive linked papers. | Explains why preferences are contextual and comparative, how reward models compress rich judgment into proxies, why current-distribution feedback and meaningful contrast matter, how process and outcome supervision differ, and how evaluator bias and over-optimization break apparent progress. Praxis transfers the feedback discipline while rejecting scalar reward optimization, model-weight updates, token-level KL analogies, and mandatory evaluation machinery. |
| [Algorithm Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14215) | Primary research paper; reviewed at mechanism level. | Trains a causal sequence model on multi-episode learning histories so a fixed-parameter policy can improve from observations, actions, and rewards in context. It establishes exploration, cross-episode context, and credit assignment as substantive requirements; it does not show that an ordinary coding-agent conversation automatically performs ICRL. |
| [RL²](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02779) and [in-context temporal-difference learning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Pj06mxCXPl) | Primary meta-RL and theoretical research; reviewed at distinction level. | Supplies the lineage in which a trained fixed policy executes a fast learning algorithm through recurrent state or context. Meta-RL is the outer training framework; ICRL names the fixed-weight inference-time adaptation mechanism rather than weight-updating task learning. |
| [Reward Is Enough: LLMs Are In-Context Reinforcement Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06303) and its [project page](https://lil-lab.github.io/icrl/) | Primary LLM-agent research; reviewed in depth. | Repeatedly places prior trajectories and scalar rewards into a fixed LLM's prompt and observes inference-time improvement. Its failures without adequate exploration support Praxis's explicit probe and alternative-generation methods, while its scalar experimental rewards are much narrower than the multidimensional evidence and human judgment in software engineering. |
| [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366) and [Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651) | Primary LLM-agent research; reviewed at mechanism level. | Shows adjacent fixed-weight adaptation through verbal memory or iterative feedback. These methods help explain learning messages and retained lessons, but iterative critique alone is not automatically ICRL and can repeat the same blind spot without external evidence or exploration. |
| [Agentic Context Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618), its [repository](https://github.com/ace-agent/ace), and [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457) | Primary context-optimization research and implementation; reviewed at mechanism level. | Uses generated trajectories, reflection, incremental contextual lessons, evaluation, and selection to improve later attempts. It supports treating context as an evolving playbook, while Praxis retains human ownership, causal artifact ownership, and authority boundaries rather than adopting one automatic prompt optimizer. |
| [Feedback loops with language models drive in-context reward hacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04549) | Primary research paper; reviewed for evaluator risk. | Shows that repeated model-evaluator interaction can optimize the proxy rather than the intended outcome. It supports Praxis's use of provenance, disconfirming evidence, multiple evaluation lenses, and human judgment instead of collapsing feedback into one score. |

## Reading rules

1. A company describing its own system is primary evidence of what it says it
   built, but its outcome claims remain self-reported.
2. Telemetry correlation can reveal a risk worth investigating without proving
   a causal mechanism.
3. A benchmark supports only the dimensions it actually grades; functional
   success, regression safety, test strength, scope discipline, style, and
   multi-year maintainability are different claims.
4. Several articles repeating the same practitioner's experience do not become
   independent corroboration.
5. An inaccessible or paywalled source remains in the provenance map, but no
   detailed conclusion should rest on content that was not inspected.
