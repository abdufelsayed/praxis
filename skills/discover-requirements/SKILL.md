# Discover Requirements

Turn consequential uncertainty into accepted meaning. Keep evidence, interpretation,
requirements, and possible solutions distinct so later work does not quietly guess.

## Establish the inquiry

Read the request, current source, existing project material, and relevant artifacts
before asking the requester to repeat context. Infer the actor, intended outcome,
current behavior, stated constraints, accepted choices, and assumptions that could
change the next decision.

Read [blind spots](references/blind-spots.md) when unfamiliar territory may hide a
consequential omission. If the available context already supports an honest next
step, state the accepted meaning and remaining limits without manufacturing a
discovery exercise.

## Work the evidence frontier

Keep one live frontier: the uncertainty most likely to change the outcome,
boundary, constraint, route, or useful next question.

1. Name that uncertainty and what it could change.
2. Choose the cheapest evidence capable of resolving or sharpening it.
3. Inspect or run that probe.
4. Separate what the evidence establishes from what it means here.
5. Promote only the supported meaning, then converge or choose the next frontier.

Use ordinary source inspection first. Reach for a supporting skill only when it
owns the answer:

- [Research](../research/SKILL.md) for a discoverable fact;
- [Interview](../interview/SKILL.md) for intent, preference, priority, or risk
  tolerance;
- [Make Tangible](../make-tangible/SKILL.md) when a perceptible artifact is the
  cheapest faithful probe;
- [Design Solution](../design-solution/SKILL.md) when accepted requirements
  already constrain one consequential shape choice;
- [Security](../security/SKILL.md) when an attacker-focused unknown could change
  a constraint or proof obligation.

Read [reference interpretation](references/reference-interpretation.md) when an
existing product, implementation, document, image, or behavior carries richer
meaning than a verbal description.

After every probe, classify the result as:

- **evidence** — what the source or observation directly establishes;
- **project interpretation** — what that evidence means for this outcome;
- **candidate requirement** — the narrow supported need or constraint;
- **remaining uncertainty** — what is unresolved, who owns it, and whether it is
  safe to carry;
- **solution implication** — a possible later shape, not accepted meaning.

The promotion chain is `evidence -> supported interpretation -> consequential
need or constraint -> accepted requirement`. A human owns any link based on
intent or preference. An implementation pattern never promotes itself.

## Converge

Converge when later specification can proceed without guessing. Present the
current requirements, their provenance, meaningful exclusions, and uncertainty
safe for a named later owner. Requirements should be independently understandable
and separable when they could be revised or proved independently.

Keep short discovery in conversation. Persist a compact discovery record under a
relevant `.praxis` effort only when the requester asks or a named future consumer
needs cross-session continuity. Use Plannotator only when the requester wants a
visual review of a substantial requirement set.

Stop with accepted meaning and the appropriate next route. Do not begin
formalization, planning, implementation, or publication unless the same request
explicitly includes it.
