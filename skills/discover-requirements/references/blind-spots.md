# Blind-Spot Discovery

Load this reference only when unfamiliar consequential territory may hide a
question the current inquiry cannot yet name. Discover Requirements keeps the
inquiry, synthesis, routing, and stopping decision.

## Find the few consequential omissions

Start with the request, current source, available References, and the requester's
stated experience. Separate:

- intent and constraints already expressed;
- uncertainty the requester already recognizes;
- territory neither party has inspected;
- assumptions embedded in the current framing; and
- facts the repository, environment, or external evidence can establish.

Inspect enough of the relevant domain, codebase, prior behavior, and external
constraints to expose omissions grounded in evidence. Look for an omission
that could change the actor or outcome, a system/trust/data boundary,
feasibility or compatibility, failure or recovery, useful solution space, next
evidence class, or whether the work should proceed.

Prefer a few supported omissions over a generic checklist. A common risk earns
attention only when this territory gives it consequence. Unknown unknowns are
not a reason to produce an exhaustive inventory.

## Assign a fitting resolver

For each surfaced blind spot, state what may be missing, the evidence that
exposed it, why it could change the judgment, who or what can resolve it, and
the cheapest useful probe. Use the evidence source that owns the answer:

- human judgment for intent, preference, tolerance, and priority;
- repository inspection for local behavior and ownership;
- external Research for authoritative or current outside facts;
- Reference comparison for rich existing examples; or
- empirical probing when behavior must be observed.

This prevents interviewing the requester about facts they cannot know or
researching a preference only they can supply.

## Stop proportionately

Stop when consequential blind spots affecting the next judgment are exposed
and assigned to a fitting resolver, resolved strongly enough to proceed, or
visible because resolving them now would not change the next decision. New
unknowns may emerge later and return through ordinary causal learning.

Return the few consequential omissions, their evidence and consequence, the
fitting resolver and cheapest probe, what territory was inspected, and what is
safe to carry. Discover Requirements decides what becomes accepted meaning.
