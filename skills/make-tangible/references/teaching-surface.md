# Teaching Surface

Load only when the caller supplies one settled model and bounded learning
objective that needs a constructed diagram, walkthrough, simulation,
demonstration, annotated view, or quiz. The caller owns the objective,
sequence, interpretation, and comprehension judgment.

## Construct a faithful lesson surface

Receive the learner's starting point, authoritative model/evidence, boundaries,
failures and uncertainty, conceptual sequence/questions, intended medium, and
lifetime. Choose the smallest faithful form: diagram for relationships/flow,
navigable view for evidence, walkthrough for behavior, simulation for state
change, demonstration for observed behavior, or quiz when supplied questions
need interaction. Conversation or a short example wins when it already teaches
the model.

The surface's primary judgment is comprehension of the caller-supplied settled
model. Ask only what is unclear, misleading, missing, or incorrectly understood
and keep every prompt, control, and call to action inside that reaction. Do not
use the surface to discover a requirement or preference, choose a solution,
approve a proposal, accept a result, or advance the caller's caller. Return any
such requested prompt as an ownership mismatch.

Trace consequential claims to accepted meaning, current source, or observed
evidence. Keep terminology, examples, transitions, and edge cases consistent;
label inference, simulation, omission, and placeholders. Return contradictory
or missing inputs to the caller instead of repairing governing truth.

For quiz mechanics, present supplied questions and evidence-backed feedback,
capture answers, and permit reasoning-focused retry without scores, learner
profiles, leaked answers, or implementation trivia. A result never proves an
implementation, accepts a record, or grants authority.

Understanding, experience, inspection, observation, or proof may support the
surface when they serve the supplied objective; this pattern remains primary
while a constructed teaching surface is the judgment.

Verify objective/starting point, evidence, medium, interactions, accessibility,
edge cases, boundaries, retry behavior, fidelity/lifetime, and export of exact
version and reaction. Return version, form, objective, evidence, answers or
reaction without pedagogical interpretation, limits, lifetime, and missing or
contradictory inputs. The caller decides what to explain next.
