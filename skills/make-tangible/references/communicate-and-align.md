# Communicate and Align

Load only when a named audience needs an informed response to a decision,
proposal, result, status, or request. Alignment means shared context for an
informed position, not consensus.

## Follow the audience's decision path

Adapt vocabulary, assumed background, order, examples, depth, navigation, and
requested response without changing governing claims, evidence, tradeoffs, or
uncertainty. Make the requested decision/response, recommendation/result/state,
direct evidence or demonstration, rationale/tradeoffs, limitations/risks/
disagreement, and deeper sources discoverable in the order useful to that
audience. Do not reproduce chronology unless it affects the decision.

Capture support/rejection, conditions/thresholds, objections/counterevidence,
requested changes, response owner, uninformed/unavailable decision-makers, and
unresolved disagreement. Attendance, viewing, silence, or no annotation is
not approval. Keep recommendation and human reaction distinct; polished
narrative cannot strengthen evidence.

Understanding, experience, observation, inspection, teaching, elicitation, or
proof may support the communication when they improve the audience's response;
this pattern remains primary while informed alignment is the judgment.

Verify audience, starting point, requested response, evidence, demonstrations,
semantic consistency across tailored views, rationale and change conditions,
limitations, objections, responsible people, links/media/responsive behavior,
and qualified reaction export. Return exact version, audience and response,
communicated state/recommendation, evidence, tradeoffs, reactions/ownership,
unresolved disagreement, and implication. The caller owns the resulting
decision.
