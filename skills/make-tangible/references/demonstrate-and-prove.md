# Demonstrate and Prove

Load only when explicit claims need a bounded claim-to-evidence case that a
reader can inspect. Verify, Review, Deliver, or another caller owns claim
definition, evidence generation, correctness, and acceptance; this surface
cannot certify missing proof or grant acceptance.

## Decompose and qualify claims

Break broad assertions into observable claims with exact scope, governing
source, proof status, evidence, provenance, environment/version/freshness,
limitations, unexercised behavior, and missing or contradictory proof. Identify
evidence strength: deterministic test, integration exercise, measurement,
controlled experiment, source inspection, runtime observation, demonstration,
or inference. Preserve inputs, collection time, reproducibility, and method.

Keep missing, stale, failed, and contradictory evidence beside the affected
claim. Use explicit statuses such as proved within scope, partially supported,
unproved, contradicted, or stale. A required unproved claim remains blocking;
do not average it away with percentages or green summaries. Make the exact
command, conditions, inputs, result, and source reachable where reproduction
matters. A demonstration proves only the exercised boundary.

If the surface captures human response, preserve questions, rejection,
conditions, and requests for stronger evidence separately from proof status and
acceptance. Verify claims, scope, current sources, links, versions,
environment, inputs, freshness, evidence type/strength, reproducibility,
status derivation, visibility of failures, and claim/source navigation.

Inspection, observation, experience, understanding, communication, or teaching
may support the case when they make claims and evidence easier to judge; this
pattern remains primary while bounded proof is the judgment.

Return exact version, claims and scope, per-claim status, evidence/provenance,
limitations and untested behavior, missing/stale/failed/contradictory proof,
human conditions, reproducibility, and implication. The caller decides
whether proof is sufficient or learning returns to an earlier owner.
