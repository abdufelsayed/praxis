# Coordinate Initiative

Use this when several projects share a consequential objective or decision.
Project-local requirements, issues, plans, and implementation remain with their
owners.

## Establish shared meaning

State the strategic objective and success condition, participating projects and
owners, what each contributes and needs, shared constraints, relationships,
uncertainty, and current evidence. Distinguish:

- a hard dependency;
- useful sequencing for learning, compatibility, risk, or value;
- coordination convenience that does not constrain either project.

Use Research for a bounded shared fact, Interview for a human strategic judgment,
Make Tangible for a relationship or sequencing surface, and Design Solution for
one consequential cross-project shape. Their results are evidence; the requester
owns strategic meaning and priority.

If the projects no longer share a useful objective, relationship, or decision,
recommend splitting the initiative rather than maintaining it for reporting
convenience.

## Keep navigation light

Present the shared objective, relationships, blocking edges, ordering rationale,
open strategic decisions, and recommendation in conversation. Persist a compact
initiative brief under `.praxis` only when cross-session or cross-project
navigation has a named consumer. Link project-owned detail instead of duplicating
it.

Use Plannotator only when the requester wants a visual review of a substantial
relationship map or initiative decision. Approval accepts that revision; it does
not start project work.

Stop with the current strategic judgment and project boundaries. Do not edit
project-local work, choose implementation, commit, publish, or deploy.
