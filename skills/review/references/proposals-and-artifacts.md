# Reviewing Proposals and Durable Artifacts

## Purpose fit

Assess what the artifact is meant to settle and what its named consumer needs.
An effort map need not enumerate file edits, an issue need not prescribe code
structure, and a plan need not contain evidence that can exist only after
implementation. Missing meaning that forces its consumer to guess is a material
gap; detail owned by later work is not.

## Governing truth and support

Read the accepted meaning and linked sources that constrain the artifact. Compare
claims about current source, feasibility, scope, or risk with current evidence.
Trace each consequential promise through the parts appropriate to its purpose:

- committed outcome or behavior;
- boundaries, interfaces, and invariants;
- assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, and deliberate limits;
- decisions the artifact makes or leaves open; and
- proof credible for its purpose and claim.

## Coherence and support

Look for contradictions inside the artifact, conflict with accepted meaning,
unsupported repository or domain claims, hidden scope expansion, unresolved
choices presented as settled, and proof that cannot establish the promise.
Distinguish a missing explanation from a rejected solution. A short paragraph
may satisfy several constraints, while a long proposal may omit the decision
that matters.
