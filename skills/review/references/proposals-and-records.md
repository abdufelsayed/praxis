# Reviewing Proposals and Records

## Stage fit

Assess what the record is allowed to settle and what questions belong to later
stages. A Project brief need not contain an implementation sequence, an
Evolution need not enumerate file edits, an Issue need not prescribe code
structure, and a Plan need not contain evidence that can exist only after
implementation. Missing meaning that forces a later stage to guess is a
material gap; missing later-stage detail is not.

## Governing truth and support

Read governing Requirements and the parent or sibling records that constrain
the record. Compare claims about current source, feasibility, scope, or risk
with current evidence. Trace each consequential promise through the parts
appropriate to its stage:

- committed outcome or behavior;
- boundaries, interfaces, and invariants;
- assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, and deliberate limits;
- decisions the record makes or leaves open; and
- proof credible for the stage and claim.

## Coherence and support

Look for contradictions inside the record, conflict with governing truth,
unsupported repository or domain claims, hidden scope expansion, unresolved
choices presented as settled, and proof that cannot establish the promise.
Distinguish a missing explanation from a rejected solution. A short paragraph
may satisfy several constraints, while a long proposal may omit the decision
that matters.
