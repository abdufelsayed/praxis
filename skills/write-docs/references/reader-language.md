# Reader language

Name the concrete actor who matters to the reader instead of exporting internal
workflow labels.

Choose the term supported by the document:

- **you** when the current reader performs the action;
- **reviewer** or **decision-maker** when someone judges a proposal or result;
- **operator** or **maintainer** for durable operational responsibility;
- **agent** only when the automated actor changes the reader's prediction;
- a project-specific role when the domain already has a better name.

Keep that term stable within the page. Direct task and operations guides address
the reader at the point of action. Contract reference and conceptual explanation
usually use neutral declarative prose. Change communication names the shipped
delta, affected users, migration, and limits.

Preserve the underlying responsibility but avoid repeating authority disclaimers
in every paragraph. Explain a boundary once where it changes behavior, then spend
the prose on the reader's action, result, failure, or recovery.

Before returning, reread as the declared reader. They should be able to tell who
acts, who decides, what happens next, and which conditions or limits matter
without learning the skill system's internal vocabulary.
