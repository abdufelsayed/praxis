# Compare and Choose

> **Status: accepted semantic source; target runtime projection: `references/compare-and-choose.md`.**

> Load this reference only after Make Tangible selects Compare and Choose as
> the artifact's primary pattern.

## The judgment it supports

Compare and Choose helps a human decide among credible alternatives when the
decision and its consequential criteria are understood well enough to evaluate
them.

It may return a selected option, a qualified combination, rejection of every
candidate, a deliberate deferral, or a reframed question. If comparison reveals
a missing criterion, false framing, or unresolved governing uncertainty, return
that learning to the owning loop rather than manufacturing a winner.

Use Explore and Discover instead when the human still needs to learn what
matters or what meaningful possibilities exist.

## Build a credible candidate set

Compare the smallest set that represents the real decision. Candidates should
be materially distinct, credible under the accepted constraints, and strong
enough to deserve consideration.

Include the current approach or doing nothing when either remains genuinely
viable. State why each candidate was included and disclose any consequential
option intentionally excluded. Never weaken an alternative, omit the incumbent,
or compare against obvious strawmen to make the recommendation easier.

## Evaluate the actual decision

Derive evaluation criteria from the owning question, accepted truth, and human
preferences. Separate:

- hard constraints that a candidate must satisfy;
- tradeoffs where several answers remain valid;
- contextual preferences owned by the human;
- evidence quality and maturity; and
- unresolved facts that could change the result.

Hard constraints are pass/fail boundaries rather than weighted preferences. Do
not let strong performance on one axis compensate numerically for violating an
accepted requirement.

Use scores only when the scale, weighting, and aggregation have defensible
meaning. A clear table, annotated tradeoff, experiment result, or conditional
argument is usually more honest than a pseudo-objective total.

## Keep evidence and fidelity comparable

Compare candidates under the same consequential conditions whenever possible:
representative inputs, scale, states, constraints, and measurement method.
Equal-looking rows must not conceal that one conclusion is measured, another
is inferred, and a third is speculative.

Expose evidence maturity, uncertainty, missing information, and unavoidable
fidelity differences where the reader evaluates each claim. Narrow the
supported conclusion when the alternatives cannot be tested comparably.

## Recommend rather than merely summarize

After presenting a fair comparison, recommend the best-supported option.
Explain:

- why it fits the accepted criteria;
- what it sacrifices;
- which uncertainty remains material; and
- which changed condition would alter the recommendation.

When the evidence cannot support a choice, recommend the smallest probe that
would discriminate between the remaining candidates. Neutral presentation
does not require the agent to withhold a useful view, and a recommendation does
not permit it to distort the comparison.

## Capture the real decision

Allow the human to:

- select one candidate;
- combine compatible parts;
- reject one or every candidate;
- attach conditions or thresholds;
- preserve a fallback;
- defer pending named evidence; or
- reframe the decision.

Export the result against the exact compared versions when conversation cannot
preserve it reliably. Keep the decisive criteria, conditions, tradeoffs,
fallbacks, and unresolved evidence. “Option B selected” is insufficient when
the choice depends on a performance threshold or another qualification.

The result is evidence for the caller to interpret and promote through its
own governing record and gate. It grants no planning, implementation, or other
authority.

## Compose supporting patterns proportionately

Compare and Choose may use:

- Understand and Navigate when readers need a shared model of the candidates;
- Experience or Simulate when behavior must be felt rather than described;
- Inspect and Annotate when exact evidence needs scrutiny;
- Manipulate and Export for compositional selection;
- Demonstrate and Prove for consequential candidate claims; and
- Elicit and Articulate for contextual human preferences.

Compare and Choose remains primary when the purpose is deciding among
understood alternatives rather than discovering the decision space.

## Verify before presenting

Check that:

- every candidate is credible and materially distinct;
- the incumbent or no-change option is present when genuinely viable;
- consequential exclusions and their reasons are visible;
- hard constraints remain separate from tradeoffs;
- factual claims and measurements trace to current evidence;
- candidates use comparable conditions and fidelity where the judgment
  requires it;
- unequal evidence maturity and uncertainty are explicit;
- the displayed criteria support the recommendation without hidden reasoning;
  and
- qualified, combined, rejected, deferred, and reframed reactions export
  accurately.

## Return to the caller

Return the exact artifact version together with:

- the result and its qualifications;
- the decisive criteria and evidence;
- accepted tradeoffs, conditions, and fallbacks;
- rejected candidates and relevant reasons;
- unresolved evidence or the recommended discriminating probe; and
- the condition that would change the recommendation.

The caller decides what becomes governing truth and whether the result can
advance, needs another bounded probe, or reopens an earlier owner.
