# Communicate and Align

> **Status: accepted Make Tangible pattern reference.**
>
> Load this reference only after Make Tangible selects Communicate and Align as
> the artifact's primary pattern.

## The judgment it supports

Communicate and Align helps a specific audience form an informed response to a
named decision, proposal, result, status, or request.

It reduces the work required to understand what matters without manufacturing
confidence. The surface leads with what the audience needs to understand and
decide while preserving material evidence, tradeoffs, uncertainty, limitations,
disagreement, and the requested response.

Useful situations include reviewer briefings, proposal pitches, PR
walkthroughs, status explanations, incident communication, result summaries,
and handoffs whose audience did not share the originating context.

## Design for the audience without changing truth

Adapt:

- vocabulary and assumed background;
- ordering and emphasis;
- examples and demonstrations;
- depth of technical detail;
- navigation and progressive disclosure; and
- the response requested from each reader.

An engineering reviewer may need interfaces and failure behavior. A product
owner may need observable behavior, limitations, and cost. Both versions keep
the same governing claims, evidence, material tradeoffs, and uncertainty.
Audience tailoring changes emphasis, never truth.

## Follow the audience's decision path

Normally make the following easy to find:

1. the requested response or decision;
2. the recommendation, result, or current state;
3. the most direct demonstration or decisive evidence;
4. consequential rationale and tradeoffs;
5. material limitations, risks, objections, and disagreement; and
6. deeper context and sources for inspection.

This is an ordering principle rather than a mandatory slide or section
template. Lead with a demo when direct experience is the fastest honest route.
Lead with a status and consequence when that is what the audience must act on.
Do not reproduce the chronology of research or implementation unless that
history itself affects the decision.

## Preserve informed disagreement

Alignment means the audience shares enough relevant context to state an
informed position. It does not require consensus.

Capture:

- support or rejection;
- conditions and thresholds;
- objections and counterevidence;
- requested changes;
- who owns a response or decision;
- who remains uninformed or unavailable; and
- unresolved disagreement.

Never infer approval from attendance, viewing, silence, or the absence of an
annotation. Do not summarize qualified support as unconditional agreement.

## Keep persuasion evidence-bound

The agent may recommend and argue for a position. It must also make the
tradeoffs, limitations, and evidence that could change the recommendation easy
to inspect.

Demonstrations, motion, visual hierarchy, and polished narrative can accelerate
understanding. They cannot strengthen a claim, conceal a weak comparison, or
move material uncertainty into an appendix nobody can discover.

When the communication asserts that a result has been proved, compose
Demonstrate and Prove and preserve that pattern's stricter claim-to-evidence
contract.

## Compose supporting patterns proportionately

Communicate and Align may use:

- Understand and Navigate for unfamiliar system context;
- Experience or Simulate for a direct demonstration;
- Observe and Assess for status or incident evidence;
- Inspect and Annotate for reviewer focus;
- Teaching Surface when a constructed comprehension surface is needed;
- Elicit and Articulate for qualified audience response; and
- Demonstrate and Prove for explicit assurance claims.

It remains primary when the purpose is enabling an audience to understand and
respond rather than proving a bounded claim.

## Verify before presenting

Check that:

- the intended audience, starting point, and requested response are current;
- every consequential claim and demonstration follows current evidence;
- audience-specific versions remain semantically consistent;
- recommendations expose their rationale, sacrifice, uncertainty, and
  change conditions;
- limitations, objections, and disagreement are discoverable;
- responsible people and requested decisions are named accurately;
- links, navigation, media, demonstrations, and responsive presentation work
  in the intended medium;
- captured reactions preserve rejection, conditions, and unresolved
  disagreement; and
- no viewing or silence is represented as approval.

## Return to the caller

Return the exact artifact version together with:

- audience and requested response;
- recommendation, result, or current state communicated;
- decisive evidence and demonstration;
- tradeoffs, limitations, and disagreement exposed;
- audience reactions with conditions and ownership;
- unresolved questions or uninformed decision-makers; and
- the implication for the caller's question or gate.

The caller interprets the response and owns every governing update, approval,
acceptance, or follow-up.
