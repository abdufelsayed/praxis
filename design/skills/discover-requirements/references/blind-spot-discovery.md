# Blind-Spot Discovery

> **Status: accepted Discover Requirements reference design; runtime
> implementation pending.**
>
> Load this reference only when unfamiliar consequential territory warrants a
> blind-spot pass. Discover Requirements retains the inquiry, synthesis,
> routing, gate, and stopping decision.

## The question it helps answer

Blind-Spot Discovery asks which few missing considerations could materially
change the problem, solution boundary, next probe, or ability to formalize
honestly. It is useful when the human or agent lacks enough familiarity to know
which questions matter.

It does not create an exhaustive risk register, enumerate every conceivable
unknown, or turn uncertainty into a backlog that must be emptied.

## Reconstruct the starting point

Begin with the request, current source, available References, and the human's
stated experience. Distinguish:

- intent and constraints already expressed;
- uncertainty the human already recognizes;
- territory neither party has inspected;
- assumptions embedded in the current framing; and
- facts the repository, environment, or external evidence can establish.

Do not ask the human to restate information the territory can reveal. The
starting point focuses the pass; it is not an intake questionnaire.

## Inspect before inventing questions

Inspect enough of the relevant domain, codebase, prior behavior, and external
constraints to expose omissions grounded in evidence. Look for considerations
that could change:

- the outcome or actor being served;
- a system, trust, data, or ownership boundary;
- feasibility or compatibility;
- important failure and recovery behavior;
- the useful solution space;
- the next evidence method; or
- whether the work should proceed at all.

Prefer a few supported omissions over a long generic checklist. A common risk
is useful only when the actual territory gives it consequence.

## Turn each blind spot into a useful next probe

For every surfaced blind spot, explain what may be missing, the evidence that
exposed it, why it could change the current judgment, who or what can resolve
it, and the cheapest useful investigation.

Classify the resolver by evidence source:

- human judgment for intent, preference, tolerance, and priority;
- repository inspection for local behavior and ownership;
- external research for authoritative or current outside facts;
- Reference comparison for rich existing examples; or
- empirical probing when behavior must be observed.

This prevents interviewing the human about facts they cannot know or
researching a preference only they can supply.

## Stop proportionately

Stop when the consequential blind spots affecting the next judgment are
exposed and assigned to a fitting evidence method, resolved strongly enough to
proceed, or deliberately visible because resolving them now would not change
the next decision.

Do not chase completeness. New unknowns may still emerge during Formalization
or Delivery and return through the ordinary causal learning path.

## Return to Discover Requirements

Return:

- the few consequential blind spots;
- the evidence that made each one relevant;
- their likely consequence;
- the recommended next probe and resolver; and
- any limitation in the territory inspected.

Discover Requirements decides which uncertainty to investigate, synthesize, or
carry. This reference never promotes findings into Requirements by itself.
